Air Traffic Control (ATC) Basics

Air Traffic Control (ATC) is a service provided by ground-based controllers who direct aircraft on the ground and in the air. ATC ensures safe separation between aircraft, provides navigation assistance, and manages the flow of air traffic.

ATC Services

Separation: ATC maintains minimum separation standards between aircraft. In controlled airspace, this is typically 3 nautical miles horizontally and 1,000 feet vertically below FL290, and 5 nautical miles and 1,000 feet at or above FL290.

Sequencing: ATC sequences arriving aircraft for efficient runway use and provides departure clearances for orderly traffic flow.

Information: ATC provides pilots with weather information, NOTAMs (Notices to Air Missions), and other traffic information.

Emergency assistance: ATC provides priority handling for emergency aircraft and coordinates rescue and firefighting services.

Airspace Classes

Class A airspace: From 18,000 feet MSL to FL600 (60,000 feet). All flights must operate under IFR. ATC provides separation between all aircraft.

Class B airspace: Around the busiest airports (e.g., DEL, BLR, LHR). Extends from the surface to typically 10,000 feet. All flights require ATC clearance to enter. ATC provides separation between all aircraft.

Class C airspace: Around airports with moderate traffic and radar approach control. Extends from the surface to 4,000 feet above airport elevation. Two-way radio communication required before entry.

Class D airspace: Around airports with an operating control tower. Extends from the surface to 2,500 feet above airport elevation. Two-way radio communication required before entry.

Class E airspace: Controlled airspace that extends from 700 or 1,200 feet AGL to FL179. No specific communication requirements for VFR flight, but IFR flights are controlled.

Class G airspace: Uncontrolled airspace. No ATC services provided.

ATC Positions

Tower controllers manage aircraft on runways and in the immediate airport vicinity (typically within 3-5 miles). They issue takeoff and landing clearances and manage ground traffic.

Approach/Departure controllers manage aircraft in the terminal area (typically within 30-50 miles of the airport). They provide radar vectors, approach clearances, and departure instructions.

Center (En Route) controllers manage aircraft in en-route airspace between terminal areas. They provide separation, navigation assistance, and handoffs between sectors.

Clearances and Instructions

ATC clearance: A specific authorization for an aircraft to proceed under specified conditions. A clearance includes elements such as aircraft identification, clearance limit, route of flight, altitude, and any additional instructions.

ATC instruction: A directive from ATC that requires compliance. Examples include "turn left heading 270," "descend and maintain 3,000," and "reduce speed to 250 knots."

ATC advisory: Information provided by ATC that is advisory in nature, such as weather reports and traffic advisories.

Pilot Responsibilities

Pilots must maintain two-way radio communication with ATC in controlled airspace.

Pilots must comply with ATC clearances and instructions unless safety requires deviation.

Pilots must report any inability to comply with a clearance or instruction.

Pilots must maintain awareness of other traffic and avoid collisions (see and avoid).
